Restoration of peat strips in the Molenpolder

Tijhuis Ingenieurs is currently involved in a unique project: the restoration of peat strips in the Molenpolder. Approximately 1,000 metres of peat strip will be reconstructed. For this, rows of piles, geotextile and soil are being installed. The banks will soon lie partly under water and will fall dry at low water levels. This allows terrestrialisation to take place and gives nature the chance to restore the low-lying peat.

In addition to restoring the peat strips, the project aims to improve water quality. This will be achieved partly by restoring the peat strips and partly by removing the thick, phosphate-rich silt layer.

Natura 2000 area

The nature reserve is part of Natura 2000 and is characteristic of the Dutch landscape. Peat has not been extracted for a long time, as a result of which the variety in this water-rich area is disappearing. Because the peat strips have lost their original function, they have not been mown for a long time, allowing trees and shrubs to establish themselves. Through the effect of the wind, more and more trees have blown over, with their roots pulling large parts of the peat subsoil with them. Over the years, larger continuous stretches of open water have thus emerged, where wave action also caused silt particles to be stirred up. This stirring up, combined with the thick eutrophic silt layer that has formed, creates poor conditions for water quality.

Until 2000, the Molenpolder was directly influenced by untreated supply water from the Utrechtse Vecht. In the summer months, when a lot of water was let in, this supply water often consisted of more than 80% effluent from wastewater treatment plants. By hydrologically separating the nature reserve from the polder area and by supplying alternative dephosphated water from the Loosdrechtse Plassen, water quality has since improved considerably.

Restoration of peat strips

The restoration of the peat strips has two goals:

  • to let the inlet water flow into the area via a longer supply route, thereby establishing a spatially stable gradient from clean, area-specific seepage water and precipitation to the inlet water;
  • the restored peat strip profile provides additional bank length, structure and shelter. These are prerequisites for the onset of terrestrialisation from the bank or from aquatic vegetation.

Surprisingly, there is a sandy bed present at a depth of a few metres. This makes it possible to build a solid structure.

Dredging work

The aim of the dredging work is to improve water quality in the Maarsseveenseplas and the Visplas. For this purpose, a total of 6 ha of peat pits will be dredged, removing approximately 24,000 m3 of dredged sediment. The dredged sediment will be stored in grassland depots.

Improving the function of the water system

Removing the phosphate-rich silt layer makes the water less eutrophic. Restoring the peat strips reduces the impact of wind and waves, which reduces turbidity of the water. Together, this improves water quality.

Restoration of the peat strips forms a basis for the formation of new peat. Improved water quality gives rare vegetation the chance to recover and expand. Increasing the natural value is a goal of the LIFE project. The emphasis here is on the following habitat types:

  • Charophyte waters (H3140);
  • Lakes with water soldier and pondweeds (H3150);
  • Quaking fen (H7140);
  • Sawgrass marshes (H7210*).

Tijhuis's work

Tijhuis Ingenieurs has been commissioned to carry out the following work:

  • Final design of the legakker (dredged-sediment drying ridge);
  • Permit assessment;
  • Drafting the specification;
  • Project management and supervision (UAV).

Tijhuis Ingenieurs is carrying out the work as a subcontractor of Movares for the client Staatsbosbeheer. The contractor carrying out the dredging work and the restoration of the legakkers is Van Aalsburg Griendhouthandel. The project duration is 2 years. The dredging work and the construction of the legakkers must be completed and handed over on 15 March 2018. The pasture depots must be finished and handed over by 30 September at the latest.

Funding

The project in the Molenpolder is part of the restoration programme 'Nieuw leven in het Veen' (New Life in the Peat), through which Staatsbosbeheer and Natuurmonumenten are working to restore low-lying peatland areas. A European LIFE grant has been awarded for the programme, and a financial contribution from central government, the provinces and the water boards is available.
