Utrechtse Heuvelrug: from mapping watercourses to targeted surface water management

 

01 Project › 02 Management › 03 Water quality › 04 Nature › 05 Result ›

The project:

Good surface water management starts with up-to-date insight. Especially in a municipality with many different ditches, ponds and watercourses, it is important to know where maintenance is needed, where water quality is under pressure and where there are opportunities to strengthen the water system.

For the municipality of Utrechtse Heuvelrug, Tijhuis Ingenieurs mapped 778 watercourses within this project. Together they amount to approximately 80 kilometres of surface water. The aim was to define the maintenance task clearly and translate it into a practical plan for management, improvement and execution.

The scope went beyond dredging alone. In addition to water depth and maintenance condition, water quality and opportunities for creating nature-friendly banks were also taken into account. This resulted in a plan that enables the municipality to work in a targeted way towards cleaner water, better management and more nature along the water's edge.

From reactive maintenance to targeted management

Water management often becomes visible when problems arise: a watercourse silts up, flow decreases or water quality deteriorates. For the municipality of Utrechtse Heuvelrug, the need was precisely to be able to steer earlier and in a planned way.

Tijhuis Ingenieurs systematically mapped the watercourses within the project scope. This provided insight not only into the current maintenance situation, but also into the priorities for management and maintenance in the coming years.

This approach makes it possible to plan maintenance better, combine measures logically and underpin decisions with up-to-date data from the field.

778 watercourses assessed

Within the project, 778 watercourses were assessed. These covered a wide range of situations: ditches, ponds and watercourses in built-up areas, along roads, near homes and in green areas.

For each watercourse, the condition of the water system was examined. Among other things, the amount of dredged sediment, the available water depth, the flow and possible bottlenecks were mapped. We also assessed where measures are needed to improve management, storage capacity or water quality.

The results were translated into 15 dredging clusters. By clustering watercourses logically, the municipality can phase and prepare the work more effectively.

More than a dredging plan

A key principle within this project was that the task is broader than removing sludge. Dredging remains an important part of maintenance, particularly when watercourses become too shallow or flow is reduced.

At the same time, dredged sediment does not tell the whole story about the quality of the water system. That is why water quality measurements were also included, such as clarity, oxygen and nutrients. This data helps to better understand why a watercourse at a particular location does or does not function well, or has poor water quality.

This creates a more complete picture of the task at hand. Sometimes the solution lies in dredging, sometimes in adjusted management, but often in a combination of measures.

Opportunities for nature-friendly banks

In addition to maintenance and water quality, we looked at locations where nature-friendly banks offer good potential. At its core, a nature-friendly bank is a more gradual transition between land and water. This creates more space for aquatic plants, insects, fish and other animals. The different habitats, from dry to increasingly wet, are of particular value in this respect.

Not every location is suitable for construction. There must be sufficient space, the design must be technically feasible and management must remain achievable in the longer term. For this reason, each location was assessed to determine where construction is realistic and where the ecological added value can be greatest.

In total, 14 promising locations for nature-friendly banks were identified, and an initial design was developed for a number of them. This gives the municipality insight into places where maintenance and ecological improvement can reinforce each other.

Coordination with the surroundings

Watercourses lie in the middle of the living environment: alongside gardens, roads, walking routes, green areas and places that residents pass every day. It is therefore important that measures are not only technically well founded, but also fit within their surroundings.

Within this project, the plans were discussed with the residents and interest groups involved. Local knowledge, points of attention and questions could therefore be taken into account at an early stage.

This coordination contributes to a more careful plan and better preparation for implementation. It reduces the likelihood of surprises and makes it clearer what the measures mean on the ground.

A practical basis for implementation

All information from the surveys, measurements and analyses has been brought together in a plan for management and implementation. It links maintenance, dredging work, water quality and nature-friendly banks.

For the municipality, this provides a framework for prioritising and planning work. Which watercourses need attention first? Which measures can be combined? And where are the opportunities not only to maintain the water system, but also to improve it?

For Tijhuis Ingenieurs, the added value lies in connecting data, field knowledge and feasibility. Not only recording what is out there, but above all translating what that means for the next step.

Step by step towards cleaner water

The improvement plan for Utrechtse Heuvelrug shows that good water management goes beyond periodic maintenance dredging. By looking at water depth, water quality, management and ecological opportunities together, a better substantiated approach to surface water emerges.

For the municipality, the plan forms a practical basis for getting started in a targeted way. Over a period of ten years, the entire municipal portfolio will be addressed; that is, all watercourses for which the municipality has a maintenance responsibility.

Where necessary, dredged sediment can be removed. Where opportunities exist, nature-friendly banks can be created. And where more insight is needed, targeted monitoring or adjustment can take place. There is also an overview of the expected costs involved, so that budget can be set aside in good time.

In this way, the municipality of Utrechtse Heuvelrug is working step by step towards cleaner water, better management and more nature along the water's edge. Tijhuis Ingenieurs supports this with research, advice and an approach that translates the survey into future-oriented water management.

More projects in this field

Working together for clean water in ’t Hol

In the ’t Hol nature reserve, we are removing 200,000 m³ of dredged sediment from the ditches to make the water cleaner and give space to plants such as water soldier and water lilies. Tijhuis Ingenieurs supervises the nature-friendly execution from the water. Through better flow and a thin layer of iron sludge, we are improving water quality and limiting algal growth. The project runs until the summer of 2027, after which nature can recover.
