Dredging work in Súdwest-Fryslân 2025-2026: from multi-year planning to completion

01 Project › 02 Preparation › 03 Execution › 04 Sustainable › 05 Result ›

The project:

At first glance, dredging work seems straightforward: there is silt in the watercourse and it needs to be removed. In practice, a good dredging project requires much more than execution alone. Which sections are really due? How much dredged sediment is there? What is its quality? Where can the material go? And how do you make sure residents, farmers, the contractor and the client all know where they stand?

In 2025–2026, Tijhuis Ingenieurs supervised a dredging round spread across 20 villages for the municipality of Súdwest-Fryslân. The project arose from the municipality's W.I.T. Multi-year Planning. From that planning, we translated the maintenance task into a project that could be carried out in the field.

In total, the project covered approximately 13 kilometres of watercourse and roughly 18,000 m³ of dredged sediment. The aim was to restore watercourses to their required depth and improve drainage, so that water can continue to be supplied and discharged properly. At the same time, the project provided scope to take a smarter approach to the processing of dredged sediment, to coordination with landowners and to recording data for future maintenance.

Preparation as the basis for implementation

The W.I.T. Multi-year Planning sets the direction for maintenance: which watercourses will need attention in the coming years? But before work starts, it must be checked in the field whether the situation still matches the plan.

That is why Tijhuis Ingenieurs began with additional depth soundings. These gave us a picture of the current depth of the watercourses. Some sections turned out to be still deep enough or contained little to no silt, and were dropped. For the remaining watercourses, we determined how much dredged sediment needed to be removed.

Defining the scope up front prevents unnecessary and inefficient dredging work. After the soundings, the investigations, preparation and tendering could focus on the sections where maintenance was genuinely needed.

From planning to an executable assignment

With the final scope in place, the high-level planning became a concrete assignment with clear sections, quantities and starting points for implementation. On that basis, the necessary investigations could be carried out, agreements made with landowners and the work detailed in a RAW specification.

Many watercourses run alongside agricultural plots. Tijhuis Ingenieurs therefore mapped out in advance where dredged sediment could be processed on adjacent plots and which agreements were needed for this. This takes time in the preparation phase, but pays off considerably during implementation: landowners know in advance what will happen, the contractor receives a clear work package and the client avoids surprises.

Water bed investigations, ecological surveys and the conditions for implementation also came together in this phase. In this way, the maintenance task from W.I.T. was translated step by step into a practically executable dredging project.

Project management and supervision in practice

After tendering, the work was carried out by the contractor Jelle Bijlsma from Gytsjerk. Tijhuis Ingenieurs remained involved and provided supervision and final depth measurements during implementation.

Our role was to ensure that what had been agreed during preparation was also properly followed in the field. We checked that the work was carried out in accordance with the contract, monitored progress and quality, and coordinated any particular issues with the contractor and the client. Ecological conditions, accessibility and any damage were also monitored.

Reports and points for attention were recorded using W.I.T. Implementation. As a result, information remained available to the contractor, the supervisor, the project manager and the client, giving a clear picture of what was happening in the field.

Sustainable handling of dredged sediment

One of the strong points of this project lay in the processing of dredged sediment. Not all dredged material needs to be transported over long distances. Where quality and location allow, suitable dredged sediment can be processed close to where it originated.

In Súdwest-Fryslân, these possibilities were actively explored. By looking at available plots in the area and making agreements with landowners, part of the dredged sediment could be applied locally.

A pasture depot was set up at two locations, enabling approximately 4,000 m³ of silt to be processed locally. This saves on transport movements, transport costs and disposal costs. It is a practical solution that shows sustainability does not always have to be complicated: sometimes the gain lies in looking carefully, consulting in good time and seizing opportunities as they arise.

The final piece of the urban water transfer

This dredging work also held a special place within a longer process. Since 2016, the municipality of Súdwest-Fryslân has been working on bringing urban watercourses up to standard before they are transferred to Wetterskip Fryslân.

Before watercourses can be transferred, they must be properly mapped and brought to the correct depth. This project formed the final piece of that transfer. As such, the work contributes not only to present-day maintenance, but also to clear future management.

From final measurement to new management insight

After dredging, Tijhuis Ingenieurs used final measurements to check whether the correct depth had been reached. The new data are then processed in W.I.T. This produces up-to-date management information for the next maintenance round.

This completes the circle: from multi-year planning to implementation in the field, and from final measurement back to future management. This project clearly shows where our added value lies: not only in preparing or supervising dredging work, but in connecting data, fieldwork, coordination, implementation and management.
