't Hol: working together for clean water

 

01 Project › 02 Restoration › 03 Execution › 04 Nature › 05 Dredged sediment › 06 Result ›

Natuurmonumenten and Tijhuis Ingenieurs are working on the restoration of the 't Hol nature reserve. And it is no simple task. Poor accessibility, soft peat soil, narrow watercourses and small strips of land make the execution technically and organisationally complex.

The reason lies in the water. 't Hol was known for clean seepage water and special aquatic plants, but the influence of that seepage water has declined sharply due to the construction of polders and development in the surrounding area. Less seepage water reaches the area, and in dry periods nutrient-rich water is brought in from outside.

As a result, nutrients have accumulated in the sludge layer. This is at the expense of aquatic plants such as water soldier and water lilies, while the invasive alien species parrot's feather thrives in these nutrient-rich conditions.

To improve water quality, the nutrient-rich dredged sediment is being removed and the water management is being adjusted. This keeps clean rainwater and seepage water in the area for longer and limits the intake of nutrient-rich water from outside as far as possible.

Restoration of the water bed and water management

The core of the project is restoring clear, nutrient-poor water. Dredging alone is not enough for that. The nutrient-rich sludge layer must be removed, but the supply, distribution and flow of water must also be adjusted.

Tijhuis Ingenieurs mapped the existing sludge layer and developed the dredging strategy. This took into account the quality of the dredged sediment, the ecological values in the area, the feasibility of the work and the measures needed to improve water management.

Dams and culverts are used to redirect the water through the area. Clean rainwater and seepage water remain available to nature for longer, while nutrient-rich water has less influence on the most vulnerable parts of 't Hol. After dredging, iron sludge is also applied to bind remaining nutrients in the water bed.

Execution in three phases

The project was tackled in phases. First, approximately 10,000 m³ of non-spreadable dredged sediment was removed and transported away along the N201. This part of the work had to be carried out carefully, because this sediment could not be spread within the area.

A new access road was then constructed. This road is needed to make the area accessible to heavy equipment, but also to make future management by Natuurmonumenten easier.

A large navigable culvert with a fauna passage has been installed beneath the access road. This keeps the existing navigation route for the nearby allotment gardens open, allowing small boats to continue passing under the road. At the same time, it provides good access over the top for equipment and maintenance.

The largest phase then began: the phased removal of more than 210,000 m³ of nutrient-rich dredged sediment and the adjustment of the water management system. This work will continue until 2027.

Working with respect for natural values

Working in a nature reserve of high ecological value calls for careful choices. The dredging work is spread over three years, so that no work takes place during ecologically sensitive periods and the impact on plants and animals is kept as small as possible.

The working method has been tailored accordingly. Where necessary, work is carried out from the water in order to spare the banks, vegetation and vulnerable parts of the area. Tijhuis Ingenieurs supervises safety, the environment, quality and ecology during construction.

This is not only a matter of checking that the work is carried out as agreed. It also requires a constant weighing up of what is technically feasible, what is ecologically responsible and how the execution fits the conditions on site.

Deliberate choices in a complex system

Nature restoration in ’t Hol is about the interplay between water quality, water bed, flow, plants and management. A single measure therefore never stands alone.

For example, some non-native aquatic plants are not removed completely straight away. They can temporarily help to limit algal growth until native species have returned in sufficient numbers to take over that role.

That is what makes the project so interesting in terms of content. The aim is not just to remove sediment, but to reduce the nutrient load of the system step by step and create the conditions for the recovery of its characteristic nature.

Handling dredged sediment intelligently

In a project of this scale, the processing of dredged sediment is also an important element. Transporting more than 210,000 m³ of nutrient-rich sediment away directly would require a great many transport movements and place a heavy burden on the surroundings.

Sediment depots are therefore being constructed in which the dredged sediment can dry out. The dried sediment is then used in the vicinity of the project area for land improvement.

As a result, the material stays closer to where it came from, and at least 5,000 fewer lorry trips are needed than if all the dredged sediment were transported away directly. In this way, careful handling of material, surroundings and transport is also built into the execution.

The role of Tijhuis Ingenieurs

Within this project, Tijhuis Ingenieurs is responsible for the surveys, designs, permits, specifications and tendering support. During construction, we supervise safety, the environment, quality and ecology.

This means we are involved from preparation through to execution. In an area like ’t Hol, that link is particularly important: choices made on paper must be workable on site, and what happens on site must remain in line with the ecological goal of the project.

In this way, space is gradually being created once more for clear, nutrient-poor water and native nature.

Step by step towards clean water

The nature restoration in ’t Hol shows that water quality cannot be separated from the water bed and water management. The nutrient-rich sludge layer, the inflow of non-local water, the flow through the area and the natural values present continually influence one another.

By tackling these elements together, room for recovery is created. The dredging work reduces the amount of nutrients in the system. The adjustment of the water management helps to retain clean water for longer. And the ecological supervision ensures that the work continues to be carried out carefully in an area where natural values come first.

For Natuurmonumenten, Tijhuis Ingenieurs is working on a project that is technically complex but above all ecologically important. The aim is clear, nutrient-poor water in which plants such as water soldier and water lilies once again have room to thrive.
