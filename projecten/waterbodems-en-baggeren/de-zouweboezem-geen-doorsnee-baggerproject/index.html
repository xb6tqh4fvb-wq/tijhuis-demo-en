The Zouweboezem: not your average dredging project

01 Project › 02 Special considerations › 03 In pictures › 04 Also read ›

The project:

In the Zouweboezem, Tijhuis Ingenieurs prepared dredging works on behalf of Waterschap Rivierenland. Around 70,000 m³ of dredged sediment was released in this wide watercourse. This sediment needs to be removed so that the watercourse can continue to be managed properly.

However, the Zouweboezem is not a simple place to work. The area lies within a Natura 2000 site, with reed borders, protected animals and little space to reach the water from the bank. It therefore had to be carefully determined in advance how the dredging could be carried out safely, carefully and practicably.

Tijhuis Ingenieurs brought the technical and ecological puzzle together. From accessibility, permits and protected species to depot locations and the processing of the dredged sediment within the area. In this way, a complex task was translated into a clear and workable approach.

Special features:

The Zouweboezem is not an average dredging project. The area is valuable for nature and therefore calls for extra care. Consideration was given to protected animals, among others, such as fish, amphibians, birds and small mammals.

The processing of the dredged sediment also called for a smart solution. A large proportion of the sediment is clean enough to be processed within the area in grassland depots. As a result, less material has to be transported by road. This reduces disturbance and fits well with a sustainable preparation of the work.

Working together on clean water in ’t Hol

In the ’t Hol nature reserve, we are removing 200,000 m³ of dredged sediment from the ditches to make the water cleaner and give space to plants such as water soldier and water lilies. Tijhuis Ingenieurs supervises the nature-friendly execution from the water. Through better flow and a thin layer of iron sludge, we are improving water quality and limiting algal growth. The project runs until the summer of 2027, after which nature can recover.
