Rijnland: years of work in every watercourse

01 Project › 02 Measurement › 03 Execution › 04 Surroundings › 05 Sustainable ›

The project:

Dredging work is visible out in the field: a crane at the water's edge, traffic marshals on a narrow road and a watercourse being restored to its proper depth. But before the work starts, there is already an extensive process of measuring, investigating, preparing and coordinating.

Since 2021, Tijhuis Ingenieurs has been supporting several dredging projects within the management area of Hoogheemraadschap van Rijnland. Each annual batch covers around 200 kilometres of watercourse. The aim is always the same: keeping watercourses sufficiently deep so that water can be supplied and discharged properly.

The scale and long duration make this work exceptional. Hundreds of kilometres of watercourse, thousands of cross-sections, multiple sub-areas and various contractors call for a clear overview, up-to-date data and careful recording. Choices made during preparation can become relevant again during implementation. It must then remain clear why choices were made and what information they were based on.

From measurement to dredging need

Not every watercourse automatically needs to be dredged. First it must be clear where maintenance is actually needed, how much dredged sediment will be released and which conditions affect the work.

That is why cross-sections are measured within the dredging projects for Rijnland. These measurements are used to determine where the watercourse is still sufficiently deep and where dredging is needed. Water bed investigations and checks on accessibility, bank protection, flora and fauna and other points of attention along the watercourse follow.

This creates a well-founded basis for the next steps. Not only does the volume of dredged sediment become clear, but also the conditions that must be taken into account during implementation.

Preparation and implementation connected

On these projects, Tijhuis Ingenieurs handles the preparation and remains involved during implementation. This means that field data is processed into clear starting points for the work on site.

This includes measurements, water bed investigations, ecological points of attention, accessibility, information on the surroundings and contract preparation. During implementation, we provide project management and supervision. In this way, the information from the preparation stays connected to what happens on site.

This continuity is important for projects that run over several years. What was previously investigated, agreed or recorded must still be traceable later in the process.

Every sub-area requires a different approach

The work takes place in both urban and rural environments, so no two sub-areas are the same. Sometimes a watercourse runs alongside a narrow road or cycle route and traffic measures are needed to work safely. In other places, accessibility, private plots, bridges, bank protection or the layout of the watercourse play a bigger role.

The surroundings also notice the work. Work takes place alongside homes, agricultural plots and roads that may be temporarily harder to reach. This requires good coordination and clear agreements during implementation.

The task remains the same in substance, but implementation differs from location to location. That is precisely why it is important that the preparation fits well with the conditions on site.

Careful recording over a long duration

The long duration calls for more than technical preparation alone. Many parties and disciplines are involved in the projects: the client, contractors, consultants, field teams, GIS specialists, project managers and supervisors.

For this reason, data, choices and points of attention are carefully recorded. This helps keep the project manageable and makes it possible to answer questions properly during implementation.

In large-scale maintenance, this recording is no side issue. It ensures that it remains clear what was investigated, why certain choices were made and how the work was ultimately carried out.

More sustainable implementation where possible

The dredging maintenance for Rijnland also pays attention to zero-emission dredging and zero-emission transport. During preparation, room is made for the use of zero-emission equipment.

The main goal remains keeping watercourses at the right depth and improving the supply and discharge of water. Where cleaner implementation is possible, that option is taken into account.

This is how sustainability gets a practical place within regular dredging maintenance.

Carefully prepared, long-running work

Behind every dredged watercourse lies a carefully prepared process. From measurements and investigations to preparation, execution, supervision and final dossier: each step must connect properly to the next.

For Rijnland, that means control over a large maintenance programme that runs over several years. For Tijhuis Ingenieurs, it means working on a project in which fieldwork, advice, contract preparation, contract management and supervision come together.

This keeps the dredging maintenance feasible, traceable and well aligned with practice in the field.
