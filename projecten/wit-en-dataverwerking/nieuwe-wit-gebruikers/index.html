New WIT users

Our Tijhuis Water Bed Information System (WIT) has been active at various municipalities and water boards for several years. In 2018 we provided 4 new users with their own digital environment:

  • the municipality of Woerden,
  • the municipality of Alphen a/d Rijn,
  • the municipality of Houten and
  • Hoogheemraadschap de Stichtse Rijnlanden.

Maintenance condition

The current maintenance condition of the water system is the starting point for the management plan. This is determined by comparing the current water depth and silt layer thickness with the data in the water board's register of watercourses (legger). Based on criteria, the WIT system calculates the year in which the watercourse will no longer meet the requirements set out in the legger. This is a handy tool for the consultant drawing up the management plan.

Setting up the system – basic data

To set up the WIT environments, the legger data (geographical lines with information on, among other things, the minimum water depth) are mapped. We then check whether sufficient data is available on the actual profile of the watercourses. This means that at least one cross-section must be known for every line segment of a watercourse. Where possible, use is made of soundings from earlier water bed investigations or dredging works. Depending on age and accuracy, it is sometimes decided to survey a new set of profiles. If the profile was measured several years ago, the WIT system itself calculates the current silt thickness using an (entered) accretion rate.

Setting up the system – assessing data

Because the WIT system already gives an indication of the dredging year, the consultant can get to work on the planning in a targeted way. Depending on the client's wishes, planning can be based on, for example, a maximum budget or a minimum water depth. With this in mind, the consultant groups the watercourses into clusters.
Watercourses are usually clustered geographically, which is practical for execution. We then check whether WIT has determined the dredging year correctly. To do this, the profile images are reviewed and assessed. The clusters are adjusted where necessary.

Setting up the system – planning

The final planning is often still determined by execution costs. Based on experience figures and information from the client, execution methods and processing methods are defined. Cost benchmarks are linked to these. These costs are entered per cubic metre of silt to be dredged. This gives a quick view of what dredging costs per year. When the dredging year is changed, W.I.T. uses the accretion rate to calculate the new volumes and associated costs. In this way, the consultant and client can quickly optimise a plan and set it up as desired.
