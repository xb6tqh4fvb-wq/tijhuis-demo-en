Ecological quick scan from the air

Map natural values, banks and surrounding features early and carefully

Work in rural areas requires careful preparation, for example when dredged sediment can be applied locally in a pasture depot. A clear picture of the natural values present is indispensable here. With specialist drone surveys, Tijhuis Ingenieurs maps these quickly, in a targeted way and with minimal disturbance.

From the air we see more than we can from the ground. With a professional drone, a high-quality camera and thermal imaging technology, we can also examine hard-to-reach or remote areas accurately, without having to walk directly through the site. This helps with ecological quick scans, follow-up surveys and making choices for planning, permitting and implementation.

The benefits of drone surveys

✔ Less disturbance
Surveying from the air provides insight without placing unnecessary pressure on the area.

✔ Better overview in less time
Even large or hard-to-reach areas are clearly mapped.

✔ Visibility of what you would otherwise miss
Thermal imaging and detailed images reveal natural values that are difficult to see from the ground.

✔ Stronger project preparation
The results support decisions on planning, permitting and implementation.

✔ Widely applicable in the field
Added value from fauna surveys to bank inventories.

 

From image to usable insight

Drone surveys provide an additional perspective on the area. Not as a replacement for ecological knowledge or fieldwork, but as a valuable complement to them. From the air, patterns, structures and points of attention become visible that are less noticeable from the ground.

The images do not automatically tell you what is needed. Our ecologists assess what they see, link it to legislation and regulations, and translate the findings into sustainable advice for preparation, planning and implementation.

With the drone, we can view an area up close without having to stand right in the middle of it. This makes the survey careful and often reveals more than we can observe from the ground.

One important application is research into hare populations. Particularly when the hare is a protected species, as in the province of Utrecht, specific population research may be required. Using thermal imaging from the air, we map where hares are present and how they use the area.

This information helps to assess what is possible. Can hares move to suitable plots in the surrounding area? Is sufficient habitat available? Or are additional measures needed to carry out the work carefully? In this way, the survey forms an important basis for decisions on planning, permitting and implementation.

From dredging task to carefully managed depot

In dredging projects, it is often desirable to process dredged sediment locally in a pasture depot. This contributes to more sustainable execution, because transport movements and CO₂ emissions are reduced.

Before such a location can be used, it must be clear which rules apply and which natural values are present. That is why we start with an ecological quick scan. Based on this, we determine whether further investigation is needed.

This gives early insight into what is possible, what is required and what needs to be taken into account in the planning.

Starting on time prevents delays

Ecological surveys depend on timing. Many species have fixed survey periods. For hare surveys, for example, two survey rounds are needed at different times of the year, with sufficient time in between. This is followed by data processing, reporting and, if necessary, a permit application.

Those who start early retain more flexibility in the planning. This allows survey results to be incorporated into the preparation, and works to be adapted, where necessary, to the vulnerable periods of species.

Frequently asked questions

When is an ecological quick scan needed?
An ecological quick scan is needed when works may affect existing natural values or protected species. For example, in dredging projects, the set-up of a depot location or works in rural areas.

Can drone surveys be used for a pasture depot?
Yes, a drone survey can help to map a plot quickly and carefully. This is valuable when a location may be used as a local depot for dredged sediment, or when the area is difficult to assess fully from the ground.

What does a hare survey using a drone deliver?
Using thermal imaging from the air, we map where hares are present and how they use the area. This information helps to assess whether works are possible, whether hares can move away, and whether additional measures are needed.

Does a drone survey replace fieldwork?
No. A drone survey is a tool within ecological research. Our ecologists assess the images, link them to legislation and regulations, and translate the results into practical next steps.

What can drone surveys be used for?
Drone surveys can be used for, among other things, fauna surveys with thermal imaging, bank inventories, floating layers, hard-to-reach areas, aquatic plant coverage, orthophotos and 3D models. For each project, we assess whether using a drone suits the location, research question, planning, weather conditions and airspace regulations.

Why is it important to start on time?
Many species have fixed survey periods. If further investigation is needed, this can affect the planning, reporting and permit application. Starting early leaves more room to prepare the works carefully.

Is this relevant to your project?

A drone survey can add value when:

  • you are planning works in rural areas;

  • protected species may be present;

  • an area is difficult to reach from the ground;

  • you want early insight into ecological points of attention, banks and/or environmental characteristics;

  • planning, permits and execution need to be well aligned.

Not sure whether this applies to your project? We would be happy to help you assess it.
