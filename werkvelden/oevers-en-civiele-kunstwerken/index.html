Banks and civil engineering structures

 

As a consultancy, we specialise in work in and around the water.

As the boundary between water and land, the bank makes an important contribution to biodiversity. We strive for nature-friendly banks wherever possible, but a bank structure is still regularly necessary. We make sure that the design of banks and civil engineering structures always fits in with its surroundings as well as possible. For example, we opt for natural materials such as willow fascine screens or vegetated sheet piling. The ecological value of the area is always a fixed consideration in our designs.

Nature-friendly banks
Banks are important for the amenity value and biodiversity of urban waters. We therefore look for opportunities to construct nature-friendly banks. We also design nature-friendly banks and supervise their construction. Nature-friendly banks contribute to:

  • Improving chemical quality
  • Increasing amenity and recreational value
  • Increasing the water storage capacity
  • Increasing biodiversity (of the waters and of the city)

In addition, nature-friendly banks often do not require bank structures, which saves timber, plastic and metal. Better still, constructing nature-friendly banks often frees up these building materials.

Bank structures
When designing a bank structure (for example a sheet pile wall), we take into account the load on the structure and the soil composition. To determine this, we carry out boreholes and cone penetration tests. Of course, we also use existing data, for example from the Dinoloket. Using all the data, the structure is calculated and the required thickness and length of the sheet pile wall are determined. We work as circularly as possible: materials are reused wherever possible.

Civil engineering structures
Civil engineering structures are water management structures such as jetties, culverts and weirs. When installing culverts, we try to plan in wildlife passages. For a weir, we look for opportunities to install a fish ladder. We approach things from an engineering perspective, but always take the ecological value of the area into account.

Bank management plans
To tackle maintenance of bank protection in a structured way and to carry out management tasks in combination, insight into the state of maintenance is needed. Our fieldworkers assess both the technical and the functional condition of banks and revetments, check the basic characteristics and look at the opportunities for nature-friendly banks. Our advisers translate inspection data into a management plan or provide specific advice on replacing or repairing bank protection. A bank management plan provides insight into the expected costs and the prioritisation for replacing revetments.
