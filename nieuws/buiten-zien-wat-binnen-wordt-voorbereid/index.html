Seeing outside what is prepared inside

Sustainable advice in and around the water begins well before execution starts. With reliable data, a carefully drawn-up specification, clear work processes and a sharp picture of what is needed on site.

At Tijhuis Ingenieurs, we work every day on projects in the fields of water beds, dredging, water quality, biodiversity, banks, civil engineering structures and ecology. We do so on the basis of broad knowledge and with an integrated approach in which data, preparation, execution and environmental factors come together. By keeping that coherence in sharp focus, we improve our processes and the quality of our work. With that aim, our geodata and civil engineering teams went out together to three locations.

According to Michiel Klaassen, project manager at Tijhuis Ingenieurs, the value of knowledge sharing is most apparent on site: “The aim of this session was to dig deeper together. By setting insights from practice and preparation side by side, we strengthen our team and the quality of our work for clients and contractors.”

Preparation made visible in execution

The team visited three dredging sites where more than 16,000 m³ of dredged sediment is being removed in total. These locations form part of a larger project whose preparation began as early as 2019.

This preparation was extensive. With more than 4,000 profiles and over 1,250 samples, Tijhuis Ingenieurs mapped four sub-areas. A specification was drawn up for each sub-area. In total, Tijhuis Ingenieurs calculated that 279,000 m³ of dredged sediment had to be removed.

During execution, it became clear how important a careful foundation is: the quantities removed matched the earlier calculations closely. Tijhuis Ingenieurs drew here on insights from a study of silt accumulation in the period 2017 to 2019. These insights were checked and confirmed by a sample. As a result, a second full survey was not necessary: a sustainable choice in both preparation and execution.

Execution requires more than equipment

It was not only the volume of dredged sediment that showed the scale of the works. The execution itself also demonstrated how many points for attention come together during the work: electric dredging, accessibility, traffic safety, the deployment of traffic marshals and the processing of sediment.

To make these aspects visible in practice, John Sminia, site manager at Tijhuis Ingenieurs, chose these works for the knowledge session: “We deliberately chose locations where a great deal of variation in execution comes together. This allows the geodata team to see which considerations play a role in the field and what zero-emission dredging looks like in practice. That is the future of our work.”

 That is the future of our work.

Moving forward together

At Tijhuis Ingenieurs, we keep a close eye on the coherence between data, preparation, execution and surroundings. That is the strength of our integrated approach: well-founded advice that is carefully prepared, works in the field and contributes to sustainable water management.
