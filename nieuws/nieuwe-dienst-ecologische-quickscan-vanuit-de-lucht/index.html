New service: aerial ecological quick scan

With the new Tijhuis Ingenieurs drone, our ecologists carefully map natural values from the air. The drone is used for ecological quick scans and targeted follow-up surveys.

Ecologist Martijn van der Meij works with the drone in practice and explains what this new way of working adds to ecological research.

What makes the drone interesting for ecological surveys?

“The drone gives us a different perspective on an area. We can take a targeted look at larger or hard-to-reach locations without having to walk across the entire site straight away. Thermal imaging also makes animals visible that are easily missed with the naked eye.”

The drone does not replace fieldwork or ecological assessment. Instead, the images provide additional information that Martijn and his colleagues assess using their professional expertise.

“A heat source on the screen does not tell you enough on its own. We look at what is present, how animals use the area and what that means for the client’s plans. Only then can you translate the information into careful, sustainable advice.”

How was the drone first used in practice?

One of the first applications was a follow-up survey of hares at a pasture depot. Together with colleague Sil Wierda, Martijn mapped the plot from the air.

During the survey on foot we saw three hares. With the drone's thermal images, we identified nineteen. That immediately shows what this technique adds to the survey.

In the province of Utrecht, the hare is a protected species. If an ecological quick scan shows that hares may be present at a potential depot location, specific follow-up research may therefore be required.

“We don’t just look at the number of hares. We also investigate where they are, how they use the plot and what habitat is available in the immediate surroundings. Ultimately, you want to know what their presence means for the plans at that location.”

For example, for creating a pasture depot?

“Yes, exactly. For sustainable dredging work, a local pasture depot is often of great value. The dredged sediment then does not have to be transported over long distances, which limits transport movements and CO₂ emissions. Before such a plot can be used, however, it must be clear which natural values are present and which rules apply.”

An ecological quick scan is an important first step here. If it shows that protected species may be present, the ecologists determine which targeted follow-up research is needed.

“With the results, we can then advise on what is possible at that location and whether additional measures are needed for the permit and execution.”

Where else do you see opportunities?

“The drone must genuinely add something to the survey. We see opportunities in other types of fauna surveys, bank inventories, aquatic plants and hard-to-reach areas. By working with it, we are getting a better and better idea of where the technology adds the most value.”

In this way, the new service offers an additional way to carefully map natural values and to advise clients early on about their project.

Would you like to know whether an aerial ecological quick scan suits your project? Read more about drone surveys at Tijhuis Ingenieurs or contact Martijn van der Meij.

Drone surveys in brief

Drone surveys can be valuable when work takes place in rural areas, protected species may be present, or an area is difficult to reach from the ground.

With the drone, we map natural values, banks and environmental features at an early stage. This helps to better assess ecological points of attention and to align decisions on planning, permits and execution.
